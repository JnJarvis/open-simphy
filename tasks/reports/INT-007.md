## Delivered corrections

Cursor-anchored wheel zoom and right/middle drag pan now work in the imported
mechanism, preview and particle editor. Home resets source view/fit. Capture is
cancelled on focus loss. Preview outlines are clipped to the viewport. Mechanical
playback continues while dragging and on focus loss; paused placement stays paused.
Swept placement stops at enabled circle obstacles, including obstacles crossed
between pointer samples. Ground suspension drag follows a segmented arc.

The previous backend allowed small fast dynamic circles to tunnel. A new baseline
case at 100 m/s failed: the striker retained +100 m/s and crossed the other circle.
A paused drag from x=-2 to x=2 passed through a static obstacle. Both new regressions
now pass. Complete world microsteps retain the eight-step minimum and adapt to
speed and minimum nonsensor radius (travel <= one quarter radius per step), with
an explicit 4096-step budget. Excessive workloads fail visibly without replacing
the previous snapshot. Sensors, filters and connected-joint collision exceptions
remain intentional. This is bounded discrete refinement, not a universal CCD proof
for every extreme scale/speed in the scene envelope. The experimental contract/RFC
records the refined semantics; accepted particle ports remain unchanged.

Open no longer holds its callback mutex while dispatching the asynchronous native
picker (immediate callbacks could deadlock). It starts in a local executable folder
and remembers the last selected path. Source previews bypass covered particle
rasterization; paused idle frames are not repeatedly rendered. A source result
from the async dialog invalidates the frame. OS shell startup is not controlled.
Actual final native dialog visibility: 1036.02 ms first open, 659.332 ms second;
request dispatch approximately .15-.2 ms. Initial pre-local-folder visibility was
1252.75/679.142 ms; cold/warm OS caching prevents treating this small difference as
a guaranteed speedup. No claim that the complete Explorer window appears in .2 ms.
